# One-step Displacement

## Core Formula

```text
one familiar human prop + one credible cat interaction + one visible displacement = gentle absurdity
```

The cat is already the unusual element. Do not add several more.

Describe each main sticker in this order:

```text
optional human action or association → primary object → physical cat interaction → one displacement → natural expression
```

## Four Presentation Modes

- `wears`: the cat wears an ordinary item. Show believable contact around ears, face, head, or paw.
- `holds`: the cat holds or braces an ordinary item. Keep the face primary and show only the paws needed for contact.
- `operates`: the cat uses an ordinary object. Keep the face primary and show only the front paws needed for the action.
- `face_in_cover`: enlarge, wrap, or reshape one familiar object into an exaggerated shell. Center the photographic cat face in one clean opening; the shell hides or replaces the body, yet the original object remains recognizable at a glance. This mode may be obvious, graphic, and close to surreal portraiture.

Use at least two modes on a sheet. Reserve `face_in_cover` for 2–3 visual accents; do not force every prop into the same face-window construction.

## One Wrong Thing

Write the displacement as one short sentence. It should identify the only mismatch, such as the cat replacing a human in an ordinary routine, one ordinary object being used as simple clothing, or one familiar object becoming an oversized portrait shell.

- Keep the sheet's overall absurdity gentle. A face-in-cover accent may be visually obvious, but it still carries only one joke.
- Keep the object's usual name, use, and material recognizable.
- Use one primary object. Small functional parts such as a telephone cord or cap strap belong to that object and are not extra gags.
- If the concept requires a backstory, fictional role, compound object, rare material transformation, or two explanatory sentences, simplify it.
- Let expressions be restrained acting cues: neutral, mildly puzzled, sleepy, focused, slightly reluctant, or one small smile. Do not diagnose the cat from its source face.

Calibration examples include a cat making a call with a traditional telephone, wearing a black plastic bag as a hood, wearing a baseball cap, wrapping a bath towel around its head, or drinking coffee. They define the everyday baseline, not a mandatory checklist. A face-in-cover accent raises only the silhouette and portrait impact; it does not add lore, extra props, or a second gag.

## Build Nine

- Use nine distinct primary objects and nine distinct cat interactions. Add a human action only when it makes the mismatch clearer.
- Vary scale, silhouette, and the balance among wearing, holding, operating, and the 2–3 face-in-cover accents.
- Judge originality within the current sheet: each concept must be clear and visibly distinct from the other eight.

## Micro-stickers

Use six small, cat-free motifs that visually echo the main props: a receiver, cable curl, cap button, towel knot, cup lid, simple sparkle, or comparable fragment. Zero to two may be approved text labels. They need not simulate debris or carry a causal explanation.
